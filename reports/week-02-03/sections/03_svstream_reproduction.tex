\section{Reproducing the SVStream Experiments}
\label{sec:svstream_reproduction}
%=======================================================================

We reproduced the experimental study of SVStream~\cite{wang2013svstream} (Figures~3--18) with our River implementation and benchmark framework. Every difference in the input shows up in the results, so this section first states which inputs can follow the paper and which cannot, then how the open ones were fixed, and only then compares the results figure by figure.

%-----------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Inputs: What Can and Cannot Follow the Paper}
\label{subsec:svstream_inputs}
%-----------------------------------------------------------------------

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Ring-Ball and Smileface-Twomoons cannot match the paper exactly} (Figure~3 and every figure that uses them). The data were never released and the paper's figures have no axes, so guessing coordinates by eye would not make the input closer to the paper, only more arbitrary. We keep what the paper states (sizes, classes, noise ratio, order of the phases) and calibrate the coordinate scale \emph{once} against the best $q$ of the paper's Figure~15 (Section~\ref{subsec:svstream_calibration}). Matching them exactly would require the data from the authors.
  \item \textbf{The normalisation of the real streams is not stated in the paper.} It is chosen by the same one-off calibration and recorded explicitly.
  \item \textbf{KDD CUP'99 and Forest CoverType are sampled.} This run uses the first 10,000 records of each stream in every figure (Figures~14--16 and 18), whereas the paper streams all 494,020 and 581,012 records. The benchmark supports the full streams, but they would take tens of hours. Figure~18 additionally keeps its earlier setting (running z-score, $q=0.02$ / $0.15$, unscaled synthetic streams). Real-stream numbers are therefore not comparable with the paper's.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Calibration of the Open Inputs}
\label{subsec:svstream_calibration}
%-----------------------------------------------------------------------

\noindent\textbf{Coordinate scale.} A Gaussian kernel of width $q$ on data scaled by $s$ behaves like $q s^2$ on the unscaled data, so a single $q$ sweep on the unscaled stream fixes $s = \sqrt{q^*/q_{\text{paper}}}$. Ring-Ball peaks at $q^*=8$ against the paper's $9$ ($s=0.943$); Smileface-Twomoons peaks at $q^*=50$ against $11$ ($s=2.13$). Because the scale is chosen by maximising the average Rand index at the paper's defaults, the synthetic panels of Figure~15 and the average Rand index of Figures~5--6 and 11--12 are not independent evidence.

\noindent\textbf{Normalisation.} At the paper's own $q$, we compute $q \cdot \max\|\mathbf{x}_i-\mathbf{x}_j\|^2$ on the first chunk for each candidate normalisation, since Section~4.4.2 of the paper asks for $q$ slightly larger than $1/\max\|\mathbf{x}_i-\mathbf{x}_j\|^2$, and discard normalisations under which almost every kernel value is $0$ or $1$. On KDD CUP'99 only the online z-score puts this product near one ($0.99$); on CoverType only min-max leaves a usable kernel ($27.6$; with the others more than 99\% of the kernel values are $\approx 0$). The choice uses kernel statistics only, not the Rand index.

\noindent\textbf{Analysis.} Only the failure at $C=1$ reproduces: the two classes merge into one cluster as in the paper. At the paper's $C=0.25$ we get no BSV at all (173 SVs, 8 clusters), although the chunk is still clustered well (RI 0.951). The reason is the box constraint of Eq.~(5): with $\sum_i \beta_i = 1$ and $\beta_i \le C$, a sphere holds at most $\lfloor 1/C \rfloor$ BSVs, i.e.\ 4 at $C=0.25$, yet the paper shows 10 BSVs here and 14 at $C=0.3$ (Figure~8). Its $C$ is therefore probably scaled differently. Implemented literally, the box only binds once $C$ is lowered, e.g.\ to $0.04$ (12 BSVs).

\noindent\textbf{Analysis.} SVStream wins clearly on Ring-Ball (0.918 vs.\ at most 0.60) and leads on KDD CUP'99 (0.982), but trails on Smileface-Twomoons (0.762, behind CluStream, DenStream and STREAMKMeans) and is last on CoverType (0.328). The comparison also differs from the paper's through its baselines (River implementations, no StrAP, DenStream's $\varepsilon$ rescaled to the data), the Python implementation, and the memory metric.

\takeaway{Only the input parameters can follow the paper exactly; the synthetic streams can only be re-created and scaled, and the real streams here are 10,000-record samples. With the scale calibrated, Smileface-Twomoons and Ring-Ball come close to the paper's clustering quality, but the BSV mechanism of Eq.~(5) never engages at the paper's $C$, so outlier detection, the $C$ study and the summary size still differ from the paper.}
